# Statistical Honesty, Methodological Circularities, and Synthetic Caveats

This document explicitly audits methodological assumptions, circularities, and caveats across the experimental pipeline of the AI Personal Finance Advisor (v3-robustness).

---

## 1. Small Sample Size & Confidence Intervals on Real Indian Bank Data (`own_test_indian.csv`)

### Measured Data
- **Sample Size**: $N = 105$ real transaction strings extracted from Indian bank statements.
- **Model-Only Accuracy**: 53 / 105 = **50.48%** (95% Wilson Score CI: **[41.07%, 59.85%]**)
- **Model + Keyword Rent Rule Accuracy**: 58 / 105 = **55.24%** (95% Wilson Score CI: **[45.71%, 64.40%]**)

### Statistical Audit
- **Confidence Interval Overlap**: The 95% confidence intervals overlap substantially between $[41.07\%, 59.85\%]$ and $[45.71\%, 64.40\%]$.
- **Two-Proportion Pooled Test**: $z = 0.6912$, $p = 0.4894$.
- **Paired Exact Test (McNemar)**: The difference amounts to exactly 5 transactions correctly reclassified (e.g., landlord/society payments containing Rent keywords). Under a two-sided sign test, $p = 0.0625$ ($p > 0.05$).
- **Scientific Conclusion**: Because the difference (+4.76 percentage points) falls squarely within the margin of error of the confidence interval on $N = 105$, **we cannot statistically claim that keyword rules improve accuracy over the pure ML model on this test set**. Collecting and labeling a larger dataset ($N \ge 1,000$) of real Indian transaction statements is mandatory future work to establish statistical significance.

## 2. Synthetic Noise Family Sharing & Canonicalization Circularity

### Caveat
The synthetic noise augmentation pipeline (`src/canonicalize.py` and `scripts/run_phase2_noise_robustness.py`) generates domain wrappers mimicking Indian payment systems:
- UPI: `UPI/{ref}/{text}/{vpa}@okaxis`
- POS: `POS {digits}XXXX{digits} {text} MUMBAI`
- NEFT: `NEFT-{ref}-{text}`
- IMPS: `IMPS/{ref}/{text}/REF{digits}`

### Methodological Safeguards & Remaining Circularity
1. **Held-Out Noise Types**: In Phase 2, `NEFT` and `IMPS` noise patterns were strictly **held out** from training data and only evaluated at test time. The canonicalizer demonstrated robust out-of-distribution gains (+22.20% on IMPS, +12.98% on NEFT), proving that stripping prefix wrappers works even on transaction structures never seen during training.
2. **Inherent Family Overlap**: However, both the noise generator and the canonicalizer share a semantic family definition (regular expressions targeting banking prefixes like `UPI/`, `NEFT-`, and `POS`). Testing canonicalization against synthetic noise generated by related regex rules carries inherent structural overlap. Performance on unstructured or idiosyncratic real bank formats may encounter novel noise patterns not captured by these rules.

## 3. Synthetic Forecast Backtesting & Seasonal Diwali Surge

### Caveat
In `results/forecast_backtest_report.csv` and the synthetic financial profile generator:
- Monthly spending trajectories for user simulation include an annual expenditure peak in October (Month 10), reflecting typical Indian festival and Diwali expenditure surges.
- When Prophet or ARIMA models are backtested on this synthetic series, Prophet's yearly additive Fourier seasonality automatically detects and fits this surge with high fidelity ($R^2 > 0.90$).

### Scientific Conclusion
This high seasonal predictive accuracy is partly a **synthetic artifact** of the synthetic generator's deterministic October multiplier rather than organic human behavior. On real-world bank statements, festivals shift according to the lunisolar calendar (Diwali occurs between mid-October and mid-November), variable bonuses alter liquidity, and irregular discretionary purchases introduce substantial volatility that synthetic generators underestimate.
